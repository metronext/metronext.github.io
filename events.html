Metro NExT (New Experiences in Teaching) Workshop 2026

November 13, 2026 at Adelphi University Manhattan Center

The Workshop

The Metro NExT Workshop is a mathematics pedagogy and professional development workshop that is open for all graduate students, postdocs, and early-career mathematicians in the NYC metro area.

The workshop is also open to anyone who might be interested in learning about new teaching techniques and in making connections with other math educators.

The workshop is sponsored and organized by the Metro NY Section of the MAA. There is no fee to attend the workshop. Please register here!

Getting Started with Undergraduate Mathematics Research...in the Age of AI
Dr. Benjamin Gaines, Iona University

Working with undergraduates on research can be an exceptionally rewarding experience, but many new faculty aren't sure how to get started. It is our hope that this will serve as a primer on the process, with tips on everything from recruiting students to selecting a project to helping students prepare to present their work. Throughout, we will also highlight ways that the availability of generative AI tools has impacted the undergraduate research experience, and the opportunities and challenges this technology provides.

Short Bio
Benjamin Gaines is Associate Professor and Chair of Mathematics and Physics at Iona University. He is also Treasurer of the MAA Metro NY Section, and the recipient of Iona’s 2022 Brother Arthur A. Loftus Outstanding Student Research Award, and the MAA Metro NY Section’s 2024 Distinguished Teaching Award. His primary research interests include combinatorial games and math education, particularly on questions involving writing in mathematics. In addition to teaching courses throughout the math curriculum, from Mathematical Thinking to Abstract Algebra, he greatly enjoys working with students on research questions in games and statistical data analysis. He is very interested in helping learners of all levels overcome the perception that they are “not math people”.

Rethinking Undergraduate Research: From Challenging Assumptions to Expanding Possibilities
Dr. Mercedes Franco, Queensborough Community College

Calls to improve undergraduate mathematics education, particularly during the first two years of college, invite us to reconsider how students experience and engage with mathematics. Undergraduate research (UR) offers one promising way to create opportunities for students to explore meaningful questions, make connections across disciplines, and experience mathematics as a dynamic process of discovery. Yet, access to these experiences remains uneven across institutions and student populations. Drawing on experiences developing and supporting undergraduate research opportunities for students at 2- and 4-year institutions, as well as facilitating professional development for faculty across institutional contexts, this talk invites participants to consider a broader vision of UR as an inclusive pedagogical practice, examine the preparation and institutional support faculty need to make these experiences meaningful and accessible, and explore approaches to assessing their impact on students, faculty, and departmental cultures.

Short Bio
Mercedes Franco is a Professor in the Department of Mathematics & Computer Science at Queensborough Community College (CUNY) and Co-Director of the Center for Undergraduate Research in Mathematics (CURM). She has a M.S. and a Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from Cornell University and a B.S. in Mathematics from Universidad del Valle (Colombia). In 2014-2016, she served as (founding) campus director of the CUNY Research Scholars Program, a university-wide program that funds year-long, laboratory-based STEM research experiences for associate degree students. As an UR practitioner, she has mentored students from 2- and 4-year colleges/universities in summer and academic-year research using the apprenticeship and research-in-the-classroom models.
